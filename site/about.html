I have spent fifteen years delivering programmes and running the governance behind them: project plans, partner agreements, data, reporting, and the administrative systems that keep an organisation accountable.

Much of that work was in semi-government settings, where a decision had to stand up to a board, a funder and the public. I learned that a rule only works when the people who must follow it understand it, and when one person is clearly responsible for it.

I now apply that discipline to AI. An AI inventory is an asset register with a few harder questions attached: what the tool decides, what it touches, how it can fail and who can stop it. I am studying applied AI so that I understand the systems I help govern, not only the rules around them.

I build in public. My notes and tools start from the primary sources, say plainly what they do not cover, and are corrected when I get something wrong. The frameworks I use are voluntary guidance unless a law or regulator says otherwise, and I never present them as law.

I am open to AI risk and governance co-op roles from spring 2027, and to conversations with anyone doing this work.
