What I work on

Three questions every organisation using AI should be able to answer.

What AI do we run?

An AI use inventory: every model, tool and agent in use, what it is for, what information it touches, and who owns it. You cannot govern what you have not listed.

Looks like: one register, kept current, with a named owner per line.

How risky is each one?

Risk tiering that matches oversight to impact. A drafting assistant and a credit or hiring model should not get the same review, and should not get none.

Looks like: a tier for each use, and the controls that tier requires.

Which rules apply?

Canadian AI rules in plain language: what they ask of an organisation, by when, and what to do on Monday. Grounded in the primary text, never presented as legal advice.

Looks like: a short brief with the obligation, the date and the source.

Why now

The dates that matter in Canada.

Canada has no federal AI statute. The obligations arrive piece by piece, by sector and by province.

  • January 1, 2026. Ontario employers with 25 or more employees must say in public job postings if AI is used to screen, assess or select applicants. Source
  • May 2026. Ontario's Auditor General reports on AI use in the provincial government, including failures found when AI scribes were tested for procurement. Report
  • May 1, 2027. OSFI Guideline E-23 takes effect, bringing AI and machine-learning models into model risk management at federally regulated financial institutions. OSFI
  • Still open. The Artificial Intelligence and Data Act died with Bill C-27 in January 2025, and no replacement is law. Source
